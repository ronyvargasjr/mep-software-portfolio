About Me

I began my career in 2012 when I started studying Mechanical Engineering Technology at Queensborough Community College. While completing coursework in CAD, thermo-fluids, and applied mechanics, I joined Sideris Kefalas Engineers in 2013 as an intern and quickly transitioned into a full-time fire sprinkler designer. For nearly a decade, I surveyed buildings, produced engineering reports, and designed fire protection systems across New York City in compliance with NFPA 13 and NYC Building Code.

In late 2021, driven by curiosity for technology, I enrolled in Transfotech Academy's QA and software testing bootcamp. That training helped me pivot into a Software Test Specialist role at IBM in 2022, where I monitored z/OS workloads, validated documentation, and participated in Agile development processes. After being laid off during the mass tech layoffs of 2023, I returned to engineering and joined Ryan Consulting Engineers in 2024, expanding my skills into mechanical systems design, including hydraulic, heating/cooling load, and electrical load calculations.

By May 2025, I stepped away to pursue freelance work, conducting MEP field surveys and producing as-built documentation to sustain myself independently. Today, I'm seeking a stable engineering role where I can bring together my combined experience in building systems design, code compliance, surveying, software development, QA, and critical problem-solving. My path has shaped me into a versatile engineer with a strong analytical mindset and a commitment to delivering practical, high-quality solutions.

MEP Engineering

// West 103rd Street

5 Multi-Family Residential Buildings — Backflow Protection

Preliminary survey of the existing water services began August 2018. Design of the proposed installation began early September 2018. The cross-connection plans were approved by the NYC DEP October 2019 and work began in the same month. Final survey was conducted November 2019 to ensure compliance with NYC Building Code & NYC DEP.

Problem Solved

The survey showed that each system was missing a backflow prevention device and underground water meters. The solution was to propose a new lead-free backflow prevention device and relocation of the water meter for inspection.

AutoCAD Survey Backflow NYC DEP NYC Building Code

// 25-40 31st Street

7-Story, 16-Unit Mixed-Use Building — Fire Protection

A high-rise building requiring a fire standpipe service alongside the fire sprinkler service due to the height of the building. This fire protection system was designed via the hydrauilically calculated method. Using Elite fire, the software plots a graph indicating if there is enough water pressure to reach the furthest/most hazardous part of the building.

Problem Solved

Multiple surveys were conducted prior to ceiling installation of each floor providing visibility of all fire sprinkler/standpipe installation ensuring a full inspection.

AutoCAD Survey Sprinkler Standpipe Hydrauilic Calculation Elite Fire NFPA13 NFPA14 NYC Building Code

// 225 West 83rd Street

Expanded Single Apartment — Mechanical System

The combination of apartments L, M, and Z on the 21st floor required a fully new mechanical system. A detailed heating and cooling load calculations was performed to determine accurate equipment sizing based on the expanded layout. Mechanical plans were produced reflecting new ductwork, equipment, and ventilation tailored to the expanded apartment.

Problem Solved

Several rooms lacked sufficient ceiling space for traditional ductwork. A ductless floor-mounted air-handling unit was proposed as the most practical and effecient solution.
